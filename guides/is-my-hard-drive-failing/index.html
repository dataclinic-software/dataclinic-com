Is my hard drive failing? The signs, and when to stop

Short answer: Clicking, grinding or beeping noises, a drive that isn't detected or shows 0 MB, one that keeps disconnecting, or a health (SMART) report that says failing are signs of a physical fault. Stop using the drive: every hour it runs can make recovery harder, and no software can repair hardware. Slowness and occasional read errors are early warnings — copy your data off once, carefully, and replace the drive.

Signs of a physical fault — stop now

  • Clicking, grinding, scraping or beeping. Often failing heads or a seized motor. Repeated power-ons can scratch the platters.
  • Not detected by the computer, or detected with the wrong size or 0 MB.
  • Keeps disconnecting or makes Windows freeze when plugged in.
  • A SMART report that says failing.

These need a professional data recovery lab, which can image the drive with hardware built for failing drives. Data Clinic's own lab is in the UK (dataclinic.co.uk); elsewhere, choose a professional data recovery lab near you.

Early warnings — act carefully

  • The drive has become very slow, or copying stalls on some files.
  • Occasional read errors, or files that won't open.
  • SMART shows a caution, such as reallocated or pending sectors.

Back up anything important once, then replace the drive. If the data isn't backed up anywhere else, read the drive as few times as possible.

How Recovery for Hard Drives handles this

  • It reads the drive's own health report when you choose it, before anything is scanned, and refuses to scan a drive that reports failure.
  • If a drive shows warning signs, it recommends imaging it first: one careful read into an image file on another drive, so every scan works from the copy. Unreadable areas are mapped, interrupted images resume, and imaging is free.
  • If the drive reports 0 MB, returns read errors or disconnects on its own, the software says so and tells you to stop using the drive.

What not to do

  • Don't put the drive in the freezer, tap it, or open it — opening a drive outside a clean room ruins it.
  • Don't run CHKDSK or “repair” tools on a failing drive.
  • Don't keep trying different recovery programs; each scan works the failing hardware.

Questions

Can software fix a clicking hard drive?

No. Clicking is almost always a mechanical fault. Software makes the drive work harder; a lab replaces or works around the failed parts.

What is SMART?

Self-Monitoring, Analysis and Reporting Technology — a health report most hard drives and SSDs keep about themselves. Recovery for Hard Drives reads it before scanning. Some USB cases don't pass it through, and USB sticks don't have one.

My drive is just slow. Is it failing?

Possibly. Check its SMART report; if it shows reallocated or pending sectors, copy your data off once and replace the drive.

Should I try recovery software on a failing drive?

Not if it reports failure, clicks, or isn't detected. If it only shows early warnings, image it once and recover from the image.
