How we use cookies

A cookie is a small file which asks permission to be placed on your computer’s hard drive. Once you agree, the file is added and the cookie helps analyse web traffic or lets you know when you visit a particular site. Cookies allow web applications to respond to you as an individual. The web application can tailor its operations to your needs, likes and dislikes by gathering and remembering information about your preferences.

We use traffic log cookies to identify which pages are being used. This helps us analyse data about web page traffic and improve our website in order to tailor it to customer needs. We only use this information for statistical analysis purposes and then the data is removed from the system.

Overall, cookies help us provide you with a better website, by enabling us to monitor which pages you find useful and which you do not. A cookie in no way gives us access to your computer or any information about you, other than the data you choose to share with us.

You can choose to accept or decline cookies. Most web browsers automatically accept cookies, but you can usually modify your browser setting to decline cookies if you prefer. This may prevent you from taking full advantage of the website.

Our software

Data Clinic Recovery for Memory Cards (free) sends Data Clinic anonymous usage counts — for example that a scan was run, how many files it found and recovered, and whether the failure screen was shown — so we can tell the tool is working and improve it. It never sends file names, file contents or personal details. If you choose to send us photos of a failing card, or your contact details, from its failure screen, they are sent only when you press Send.

Data Clinic Recovery for Hard Drives sends nothing unless you choose to, with two exceptions: when it starts with a licence key and the computer is online, it sends that key and the program’s version number to our server to check whether it has been renewed or refunded; and when it starts and the computer is online, it also asks dataclinic.co.uk whether a newer version is available — that request contains nothing about you or your drives. Licence keys contain the licence holder’s name (and business name, for Business licences) and the order number, and we keep a record of the keys we issue with the order they belong to. It never sends the contents of your files. If you send a problem report, it includes the program’s technical log, which can mention your drive’s partition and volume names and size, the name of a file you viewed, save locations, the drive health result and your licence holder name and licence ID; you can save the report to a file and read it before sending. If you ask us to contact you from its failure screen, your message includes the drive’s model, its size and the symptoms the program found.

In both programs, a problem report from the Help menu is sent only if you choose to send it. Software purchases are sold through Onelink, the merchant-of-record service of Stripe, which processes your payment and billing details as merchant of record; we never see your card details. We receive your name, e-mail address, business name (if you give one) and order details so that we can issue and support your licence.

Ask Rex AI in Data Clinic Recovery for Hard Drives

Ask Rex is an optional assistant in our Hard Drives software. It is off until you turn it on. When you use it, the software sends what you type to Rex, the replies so far, and basic details of the job: whether a drive is chosen, its size and health, and, after a scan, how many files were found, how many are deleted or can’t be recovered, and how many you have ticked. It also sends your computer’s Windows region and the date. It does not send your files, their contents, or the list of file names. A file or folder name is sent only if you type it.

These details go to our server, which passes them to Anthropic PBC, the provider of the Claude AI model behind Rex, and returns Rex’s reply. We do not keep a copy of what you type. To apply a daily usage limit, our server keeps a count against a coded form of your internet address that changes every day and cannot be turned back into the address. Anthropic does not receive your internet address. Under its commercial terms Anthropic does not use this content to train its models and keeps it for up to 30 days for safety monitoring before deleting it. Anthropic is based in the United States, so this content is transferred outside the UK under Anthropic’s data protection terms. Our server also uses your internet address to check which country you are in, because Ask Rex is only offered in countries where Anthropic provides its service. The address is looked up in a database on our own server and is not stored or passed on. IP geolocation by DB-IP.

We use Ask Rex to help you plan a recovery you have asked for. Please don’t type passwords or other personal details into it. You can stop using Ask Rex at any time; the rest of the software works without it.
